You are standing still. Everything on this page is measured outward from your eyes, and the scale is logarithmic: every mark on the ruler is ten times the one before it.

That is the only way a single page can hold both a staircase and the observable universe. It also means the last quarter of your scroll covers more ground than everything before it combined.

Distances are best current estimates and are rounded. Beyond a few billion light-years, space itself has expanded while light was in transit, so present-day distance and light-travel time stop agreeing; the last two stops explain the split. The last few stops mix two different measures — how far the light travelled, and how far away the object sits today — which is the subject of the light-travel horizon. Voyager 1's position is extrapolated from its published trajectory and drifts slightly from the official figure. Stops sit at their true logarithmic positions except where two would collide, in which case the later one is pushed down far enough to clear the one above it.
