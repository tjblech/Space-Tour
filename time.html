The clock in this room reads the same as it always has. This page counts backward from this second, and every mark on the ruler is ten times further back than the last.

Two thirds of the way down, counting backward stops working, because everything left happens inside the first fraction of a second. The ruler starts over and counts forward from zero.

You have been here the whole time.

Every instant on this page is upstream of the one you are sitting in. The protons in your hand were assembled a millionth of a second after the beginning, the oxygen you are breathing was waste from a bacterium, and the carbon was made inside a star that had to die first.

Nothing about that arrangement was aimed at you. It simply had nowhere else to go.

Measure distance instead

Dates are best current estimates, rounded, and several are actively argued over — the first stone tools, the first stars, and anything before the first microsecond most of all. The switch from counting backward to counting forward happens at the Big Bang; before that point the ruler shows time before now, and after it, time since the beginning. Temperatures in the early universe are from the standard radiation-era relation and are approximate. Every mark on the ruler is still ten times the last, but the page gives different regions different amounts of room per decade: almost everything old is crushed into a handful of decades of ‘ago’, while the first second spans sixty decades with very little in them. Where two stops would still collide, the later one is pushed down far enough to clear the one above it.
